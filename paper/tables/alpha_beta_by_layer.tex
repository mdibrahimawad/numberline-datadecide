\begin{tablenotes}\footnotesize
\item \textbf{Models.} The 25 DataDecide 1B models (Ai2): same architecture (16 layers), same
training length (100B tokens), different pre-training corpora. One point per model.
\item \textbf{$\alpha$ (data exponent).} From the exact 100B-token stream each model was trained on,
$c(n)$ = how often the integer $n$ appears as a whole number in the text ($1\le n\le 10^4$).
$\alpha$ is the slope of the least-squares line of $\log c(n)$ on $\log n$
($c(n)\propto n^{\alpha}$; $\alpha<0$; a flatter, less negative $\alpha$ means large numbers are
relatively more frequent).
\item \textbf{$\beta$ (number-line compression).} Prompts \texttt{c1=c1,c2=c2,c3=c3,n=} with $n$
in 10 magnitude bands from 10 to 10\,000 (100 correctly answered prompts per band and seed,
3 seeds). At each layer the hidden state of the last token is projected on its first principal
component (PC1), and PC1 $= a + s\,\beta^{\log_{10} n}$ is fitted over all prompts. $\beta$ is the
factor by which the gap between consecutive decades changes: $\beta=1$ is a logarithmic number
line (every decade gets equal space), $\beta<1$ compresses large numbers more than a logarithm,
$\beta>1$ moves towards a linear line. Per model: median over the 3 seeds.
\item \textbf{Monotonicity.} Median over models of $|\text{Spearman}(n,\mathrm{PC1})|$: how well that
layer orders the numbers along PC1 (1 = perfectly ordered). $^\dagger$\,Below 0.85: no clear
number line at that layer, so $\beta$ there is not meaningful.
\item \textbf{Pearson $r$} measures a straight-line relation between $\alpha$ and $\beta$ across
the 25 models; \textbf{Spearman $\rho_s$} only asks whether a higher $\alpha$ goes with a higher
$\beta$ (rank order), and is robust to single outlying models. $p$: two-sided test of zero
correlation. \textbf{Bold}: significant after Bonferroni correction for 16 layers
($p<0.05/16=0.0031$).
\end{tablenotes}
